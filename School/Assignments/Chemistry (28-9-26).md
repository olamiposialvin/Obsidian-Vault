# Week 1
### Evaluation

**1. What is the element that is unlikely to be a metal?**
*   **Answer: S**
*   **Reasoning:** Elements with 1, 2, or 3 electrons in their outermost shell (P, Q, R) tend to lose electrons and are typically metals. Element S has 7 electrons in its outermost shell, which means it needs one electron to complete its octet. This is characteristic of non-metals (specifically halogens).

**2. The elements listed below belong to the same group in the periodic table; ${}_9\text{F}$, ${}_{17}\text{Cl}$, ${}_{35}\text{Br}$, ${}_{53}\text{I}$**

*   **(i) What is the electronic structure of the first-member?**
    *   **Answer: 2, 7** (or $1s^2 2s^2 2p^5$)
    *   **Reasoning:** The first member is Fluorine (F) with atomic number 9. The first shell holds 2 electrons, and the second shell holds the remaining 7.

*   **(ii) What is the family name of the elements?**
    *   **Answer: Halogens**
    *   **Reasoning:** Group 17 elements (F, Cl, Br, I) are known as the halogen family.

*   **(iii) Which of the elements has the strongest oxidizing ability?**
    *   **Answer: Fluorine (F)**
    *   **Reasoning:** Oxidizing ability (electronegativity) decreases as you go down a group. Fluorine is at the top, so it is the strongest oxidizing agent.

### Objective Test

**1. The period table is an arrangement of elements in order of their.**
*   **Answer: (d) atomic numbers**
*   **Reasoning:** The modern periodic law states that properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers.

**2. Four elements T, X, Y, Z have 1, 2, 3 and 6 electrons in their outmost shells respectively. The element that is likely to be a non-metal is.**
*   **Answer: (c) Z**
*   **Reasoning:** Elements with 1, 2, or 3 valence electrons (T, X, Y) are generally metals. Element Z has 6 valence electrons, which is characteristic of non-metals (Group 16, like Oxygen or Sulfur).

**3. An atom with the electronic configuration: $1s^2, 2s^2, 2p^6, 3s^2, 3d^1, 4s^2$ is that of:**
*   **Answer: (c) a transition metal**
*   **Reasoning:** Although the configuration in the question seems to have a typo (missing $3p^6$), the presence of electrons in the d-orbital ($3d^1$) indicates it is a transition metal. Specifically, if we assume the full configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6 3d^1 4s^2$, it corresponds to Scandium (atomic number 21).

**4. An element whose atomic number is 19 is**
*   **Answer: (c) An alkali metal**
*   **Reasoning:** Atomic number 19 is Potassium (K). Its electron configuration is 2, 8, 8, 1. Having 1 valence electron places it in Group 1, the alkali metals.

**5. Group 0 or 8 elements are called.**
*   **Answer: (c) noble gas**
*   **Reasoning:** Group 0 (or Group 18 in modern IUPAC numbering) elements are the noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, etc.), known for their full outer electron shells and lack of reactivity.

## Week 2
### Evaluation 1

**1. List three periodic properties of elements that generally increase across the period of the Periodic Table.**
*   **Answer:**
    1.  **Ionization Energy:** The energy required to remove an electron increases because the nuclear charge increases and atomic radius decreases, holding electrons tighter.
    2.  **Electronegativity:** The ability of an atom to attract shared electrons increases for the same reasons (higher nuclear charge, smaller radius).
    3.  **Electron Affinity:** The energy released when an electron is added generally increases (becomes more negative) across a period.
    *(Note: Effective Nuclear Charge and Non-metallic character also increase).*

**2. Explain the term electron affinity and discuss how it varies across the period and down the group of the Periodic Table.**
*   **Definition:** Electron affinity is the amount of energy released (or change in energy) when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom to form a negative ion (anion). It represents the tendency of an atom to accept an electron.
*   **Variation across a period:** It generally **increases** (becomes more exothermic/negative) from left to right. This is because atomic size decreases and nuclear charge increases, making the nucleus more attractive to an incoming electron.
*   **Variation down a group:** It generally **decreases** (becomes less exothermic/negative) from top to bottom. This is because atomic size increases and the shielding effect of inner shells increases, reducing the attraction for an incoming electron.

### Evaluation 2

**1. The atoms of four elements are represented as ${}_{20}\text{Q}$, ${}_{16}\text{R}$, ${}_{10}\text{S}$ and ${}_{8}\text{T}$. Which of the elements would be unreactive?**
*   **Answer: S**
*   **Reasoning:**
    *   Q (Atomic number 20): Configuration 2, 8, 8, 2 (Metal, reactive).
    *   R (Atomic number 16): Configuration 2, 8, 6 (Non-metal, reactive).
    *   **S (Atomic number 10):** Configuration 2, 8. This is a Noble Gas (Neon) with a full outer shell, making it stable and unreactive.
    *   T (Atomic number 8): Configuration 2, 6 (Non-metal, reactive).

**2. Explain the meaning of the diagonal relationship the periods 2 and 3 of elements in the Periodic Table.**
*   **Answer:** The diagonal relationship refers to the similarity in chemical properties between certain elements in the second period (Li, Be, B) and the elements in the third period that are diagonally adjacent to them (Mg, Al, Si).
*   **Reason:** This occurs because these diagonal pairs have similar charge-to-size ratios (ionic potential) and similar electronegativity values. For example, Lithium (Li) resembles Magnesium (Mg), and Beryllium (Be) resembles Aluminum (Al).

**2. If the electronic configuration of an element X is $1s^2, 2s^2, 2p^5$, which of the following statements about X is false?**
*   **Answer: (a) Have five electrons in its valence shell.**
*   **Reasoning:**
    *   Total electrons = 2 + 2 + 5 = 9. So, atomic number is 9 (Fluorine). Statement (b) is true.
    *   It is in Group 17 (7 valence electrons: $2s^2 + 2p^5$), so it is a Halogen. Statement (c) is true.
    *   Fluorine is a non-metal. Statement (d) is true.
    *   The valence shell is the 2nd shell ($n=2$). It contains $2 + 5 = 7$ electrons, not 5. So statement (a) is false.

**3. The electronic configuration of an element X is $1s^2, 2s^2, 2p^6, 3s^2, 3p^4$. It can be deduced that X...**
*   **Answer: (a) Belongs to group 6 of the periodic table.**
*   **Reasoning:**
    *   Total electrons = 16 (Sulfur).
    *   Valence electrons = $3s^2 + 3p^4 = 6$. This places it in Group 6 (or Group 16 in modern IUPAC). So (a) is correct.
    *   Highest shell is 3, so it's Period 3. Statement (b) says Period IV, so it's false.
    *   The p-orbital has 4 electrons ($ \uparrow\downarrow, \uparrow, \uparrow $), meaning there are 2 unpaired electrons. Statement (c) says 3 "unpaid" (unpaired), so it's false.
    *   Statement (d) says Group 4, which is false.
