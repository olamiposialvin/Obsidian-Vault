# Question 4

Define standard electrode potential.

The standard electrode potential $(E^\circ)$ of an electrode is the potential difference of the electrode when it is in contact with a solution of its ions having unit concentration $(1 \, \text{mol} \, \text{L}^{-1})$, the gas involved (if any) at $1 \, \text{bar}$ pressure, and the system is maintained at $298 \, \text{K}$ temperature.

# Question 5

What is activation energy?

Activation Energy is the **minimum** amount of energy that must be available to reactants for a chemical reaction to occur.

# Question 6

What is the significance of activation complex in a chemical reaction?

The activation complex is the unstable, highest-energy arrangement of atoms at the peak of the energy barrier between reactants and products; its energy above the reactants is the activation energy, so a lower peak (as with a catalyst) means a faster reaction. e.g. $\ce{Cl + H2 -> [Cl\cdots H\cdots H]^{\ddagger} -> HCl + H}$

# Question 8

Briefly explain what would be observed when powdered zinc $(\ce{Zn})$ is added to a solution of copper tetraoxosulphate (vi) $(\ce{CuSO4})$ in a beaker. Support the explanation with equations.

## Observations

1. The blue solution fades to colourless.
2. A reddish-brown solid (copper) settles at the bottom of the beaker.
3. The zinc powder dissolves.
4. The beaker warms up, because the reaction gives off heat.

## Equations

1. Full equation: $\ce{Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) -> ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)}$
2. Ionic equation: $\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^{2+}(aq) -> Zn^{2+}(aq) + Cu(s)}$
3. Half-equations:
	- Oxidation: $\ce{Zn(s) -> Zn^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-}$
	- Reduction: $\ce{Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- -> Cu(s)}$

## Why does it happen?

Zinc is more reactive than copper, so it gives up electrons and displaces copper from the solution. The blue colour comes from $\ce{Cu^{2+}}$ ions. As they are used up, the blue fades. The $\ce{Zn^{2+}}$ ions that form are colourless.

# Question 10

Write one function of a salt bridge.

Salt bridges keep each half-cell electrically neutral by letting ions migrate between them. Without it, charge builds up and the redox reaction stops almost immediately.
