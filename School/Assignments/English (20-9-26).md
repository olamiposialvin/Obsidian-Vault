# Non-Textbook
## 1. Reflexive Pronouns
**Definition:** Pronouns that end in "-self" or "-selves" and refer back to the subject of the clause, indicating that the subject and the object of the verb are the same entity (e.g., myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves).  
- **Example 1:** After a long day at work, Sarah treated **<u>herself</u>** to a relaxing spa massage.  
- **Example 2:** The cats were cleaning **<u>themselves</u>** in the warm afternoon sun.  

## 2. Collective Nouns
**Definition:** Nouns that represent a group of people, animals, or things as a single, unified entity or unit.  
- **Example 1:** The **<u>committee</u>** has reached a unanimous decision on the new policy.  
- **Example 2:** A **<u>flock</u>** of birds flew south for the winter.  

## 3. Proper Nouns
**Definition:** Specific, unique names of particular people, places, organizations, or things, which are always capitalized in English.  
- **Example 1:** **<u>Mount Everest</u>** is the highest mountain in the world.  
- **Example 2:** **<u>Shakespeare</u>** wrote many famous plays during the Elizabethan era.  

## 4. Relative Nouns (Relative Pronouns)
**Definition:** In standard English grammar, this category is universally known as **Relative Pronouns**. These are words that introduce a relative (adjective) clause and connect it to a preceding noun or pronoun (the antecedent), providing more information about it. *(Note: The term "relative noun" is occasionally found in comparative linguistics or older texts to describe this exact same function).*  
- **Example 1:** The scientist **<u>who</u>** discovered the new element was awarded a prestigious prize.  
- **Example 2:** I finally read the book **<u>that</u>** you recommended to me last month.  

## 5. Adverb of Manner
**Definition:** Adverbs that describe *how* an action is performed or how something happens. They often (but not always) end in "-ly".  
- **Example 1:** The musician played the piano **<u>beautifully</u>** during the concert.  
- **Example 2:** He finished his assignment **<u>quickly</u>** so he could go outside and play.  

## 6. Reciprocal Pronoun
**Definition:** Pronouns used to indicate a mutual action or relationship between two or more subjects, where each is both the doer and the receiver of the action.  
- **Example 1:** The two teammates always support **<u>each other</u>** during difficult matches.  
- **Example 2:** The members of the book club listen to **<u>one another</u>** respectfully.  

## 7. Personal Pronouns
**Definition:** Pronouns that represent specific people or things and change form depending on their grammatical person, number, gender, and case (e.g., I, you, he, she, it, we, they, me, him, her, us, them).  
- **Example 1:** **<u>She</u>** gave **<u>him</u>** the package before leaving.  
- **Example 2:** **<u>We</u>** decided that **<u>it</u>** was time to go home.  

## 8. Adverb of Place
**Definition:** Adverbs that indicate the location or direction of *where* an action takes place.  
- **Example 1:** The children are playing **<u>outside</u>** while it is still sunny.  
- **Example 2:** Please leave the groceries **<u>here</u>** on the kitchen counter.  

## 9. Intensive Pronouns
**Definition:** Pronouns that end in "-self" or "-selves" and are used solely to emphasize or intensify a preceding noun or pronoun. Unlike reflexive pronouns, they can be removed from the sentence without changing its core meaning.  
- **Example 1:** The CEO **<u>himself</u>** will attend the meeting to address your concerns.  
- **Example 2:** I baked this entire cake **<u>myself</u>** without any help.  

## 10. Adverb of Time
**Definition:** Adverbs that indicate *when* an action occurs, how long it lasts, or how frequently it happens.  
- **Example 1:** We are planning to visit our grandparents **<u>tomorrow</u>**.  
- **Example 2:** She has **<u>always</u>** wanted to travel to Japan.

## 1. Definition of Citizenship and 4 Ways it Can Be Acquired
**Definition of Citizenship:**  
Citizenship is the legal status of being a recognized, bona fide member of a sovereign state or nation. It entails a mutual relationship between the individual and the state, where the individual owes allegiance to the state and, in return, is entitled to its protection, rights, and privileges, while also bearing specific duties and responsibilities.

**4 Ways Citizenship Can Be Acquired:**  
1. **By Birth (Jus Soli):** A person automatically becomes a citizen if they are born within the territorial boundaries of a country, regardless of their parents' nationality (practiced in countries like the USA and Canada).  
2. **By Descent (Jus Sanguinis):** Citizenship is acquired through one’s parents or grandparents. If a child is born to citizens of a country, even if the birth occurs outside the country’s borders, the child inherits citizenship by descent (common in countries like Nigeria, the UK, and many European nations).  
3. **By Registration:** This applies to specific categories of people, most commonly foreign women who marry male citizens of a country (or vice versa, depending on the country's laws), or individuals born in the country to foreign parents who apply for citizenship upon reaching adulthood.  
4. **By Naturalization:** This is a legal process by which a foreigner voluntarily applies to become a citizen of a country after fulfilling specific constitutional requirements, such as living in the country for a mandated number of years, demonstrating good character, having a means of livelihood, and renouncing their former citizenship.  

*(Note: Some countries also grant **Honorary Citizenship** to foreigners who have rendered exceptional, meritorious service to the nation, though this is rare and usually does not confer political rights like voting).*

---

## 2. Five Rights of a Citizen (with 3 Explained)
**Five Rights of a Citizen:**  
1. Right to life  
2. Right to freedom of speech and expression  
3. Right to vote and be voted for (Franchise)  
4. Right to education  
5. Right to freedom of religion  

**Explanation of 3 Rights:**  
1. **Right to Life:** This is the most fundamental human right. It guarantees that every citizen has the inherent right to live, and the state is obligated to protect this life from arbitrary deprivation, violence, or unlawful execution.  
2. **Right to Freedom of Speech and Expression:** This empowers citizens to express their opinions, ideas, and beliefs openly through speech, writing, or media without fear of government censorship or retaliation. However, this right is not absolute and is usually restricted by laws against hate speech, defamation, or incitement to violence.  
3. **Right to Vote and Be Voted For:** This is the cornerstone of democratic participation. It grants citizens the power to choose their leaders and representatives through periodic, free, and fair elections. It also includes the eligibility to contest for political office, provided the constitutional requirements (like age and education) are met.

---

## 3. Five Duties or Responsibilities of a Good Citizen
1. **Obeying the Law:** Adhering to the constitution, statutes, and regulations established by the government to maintain peace and order.  
2. **Payment of Taxes:** Promptly and honestly paying legally imposed taxes, rates, and levies, which provide the revenue needed for the government to fund public services and infrastructure.  
3. **Defending the Country:** Being ready to protect the nation’s sovereignty and territorial integrity, which may include participating in national service, voting responsibly, or serving in the military when called upon.  
4. **Protecting Public Property:** Safeguarding government and community assets (e.g., roads, schools, hospitals, and power facilities) from vandalism, destruction, or misuse.  
5. **Participating in Civic Life:** Engaging in community development, voting in elections, and staying informed about national issues to hold leaders accountable.

---

## 4. Five Qualities of a Good Citizen (Explained)
1. **Patriotism:** A deep sense of love, devotion, and attachment to one’s country. A patriotic citizen prioritizes the national interest over personal or ethnic interests and is proud of their nation’s heritage and achievements.  
2. **Integrity:** The quality of being honest and having strong moral principles. A good citizen with integrity refuses to engage in or tolerate corruption, fraud, or deceit, and always strives to do what is right, even when no one is watching.  
3. **Tolerance:** The ability to respect, accept, and coexist peacefully with people who have different beliefs, cultures, religions, or political opinions. Tolerance prevents conflict and promotes national unity in diverse societies.  
4. **Civic Consciousness:** Being well-informed and aware of one’s rights, duties, and the workings of the government. A civically conscious citizen actively participates in community meetings, votes intelligently, and advocates for positive change.  
5. **Law-Abiding Nature:** A consistent respect for the rule of law. A good citizen understands that laws are designed to protect everyone’s rights and maintain social order, and therefore voluntarily complies with them without needing constant enforcement.


# Economics (21-9-26)

Before the introduction of formal currency, the Nigerian economy relied heavily on the barter system, where goods and services were directly exchanged for other items of perceived equal value. As trade networks expanded, this system naturally evolved into the use of commodity money. Pre-colonial societies utilized items such as cowrie shells, manillas, brass rods, beads, and salt as widely accepted mediums of exchange. Cowrie shells, in particular, became the most dominant and enduring form of currency across many regions, facilitating local and trans-Saharan trade long before European contact.

The colonial era marked a significant structural shift in Nigeria’s monetary history. In 1912, the British colonial administration established the West African Currency Board (WACB) to standardize trade across its West African territories. The WACB introduced the British West African pound, shillings, and pence as legal tender, systematically demonetizing indigenous currencies like cowries and manillas to consolidate economic control. This colonial currency remained in circulation until Nigeria’s approach to independence, when the need for a sovereign monetary system became apparent. Consequently, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) was established on July 1, 1959, issuing the first uniquely Nigerian pound notes and coins to replace the West African pound.

A major milestone occurred on January 1, 1973, when Nigeria decimalized its currency, transitioning from the pound system to the Naira (₦) and Kobo. This reform was designed to modernize the economy, simplify financial transactions, and decisively move away from colonial monetary structures. One Naira was set to equal two Nigerian pounds, with 100 kobo making up a single Naira. Since then, the Nigerian currency has undergone several redesigns and technological innovations, including the introduction of polymer banknotes and the launch of the digital e-Naira in 2021, reflecting the country’s ongoing evolution from ancient barter to modern digital finance.

## 1. Definition of Duties and Obligations of Citizens

While often used interchangeably in everyday conversation, "duties" and "obligations" have distinct meanings in civic education:

*   **Duties of Citizens:** These are the **moral, ethical, and civic responsibilities** that individuals owe to their society, community, and fellow citizens. They are actions that a "good" citizen should voluntarily perform to promote harmony, progress, and the general welfare of the state. They are generally not punishable by law if ignored, though they are socially expected. (e.g., voting, helping the less privileged, keeping the environment clean).
*   **Obligations of Citizens:** These are the **mandatory, legally binding requirements** imposed on individuals by the government or the constitution. Citizens *must* fulfill these requirements, and failure to do so usually results in legal consequences, fines, or penalties. (e.g., paying taxes, obeying the law, serving in the military if drafted).

---

## 2. Six Duties of a Good Citizen

1.  **Voting in Elections:** Participating in the democratic process by voting for competent and honest leaders to ensure good governance.
2.  **Respecting the Rights of Others:** Tolerating different opinions, religions, and cultures, and not infringing upon the fundamental human rights of fellow citizens.
3.  **Protecting Public Property:** Taking care of public amenities (like parks, roads, and government buildings) and preventing vandalism so they can serve the community longer.
4.  **Staying Informed:** Paying attention to national issues, understanding government policies, and holding leaders accountable for their actions.
5.  **Promoting Peace and Unity:** Avoiding actions or speech that incite violence, hatred, or division, and actively working to foster national cohesion.
6.  **Community Service and Patriotism:** Willingly assisting in community development projects, helping the needy, and defending the country’s good image both at home and abroad.

---

## 3. Five Obligations of Citizens to the Government

1.  **Payment of Taxes:** Citizens are legally obligated to pay their taxes (income tax, property tax, etc.) promptly and honestly. This is the primary source of revenue the government uses to provide essential services like healthcare, education, and infrastructure.
2.  **Obedience to the Law:** Every citizen must abide by the constitution and the laws enacted by the government. This ensures that the society operates within a legal framework and prevents chaos.
3.  **Defense of the Country:** Citizens have an obligation to defend the nation against external or internal threats. This can involve enlisting in the armed forces, serving during times of war, or assisting law enforcement agencies when required.
4.  **Respect for National Symbols and Authority:** Citizens must show respect for the national flag, the national anthem, and constituted government authorities. This fosters national pride and maintains the legitimacy of the state.
5.  **Civic Duties (e.g., Jury Duty or Census Participation):** When summoned by the court, citizens are legally obligated to serve as jurors to ensure fair trials. They are also obligated to provide accurate information during national population censuses to aid in national planning.

---

## 4. Five Reasons Why Citizens Should Obey the Laws of the Country

**1. To Maintain Peace and Order**
*Explanation:* Laws act as a code of conduct that guides human behavior. When citizens obey laws—such as traffic rules or criminal codes—it prevents anarchy, reduces crime, and ensures that society remains peaceful, orderly, and safe for everyone to live in.

**2. To Protect Fundamental Human Rights**
*Explanation:* The primary purpose of many laws is to protect the rights and liberties of individuals from being abused by others or by the government. By obeying the law, citizens ensure that everyone’s right to life, property, and freedom is respected. If laws are broken, people's rights are violated.

**3. To Avoid Legal Sanctions and Punishments**
*Explanation:* Disobeying the law attracts negative consequences from the state, such as heavy fines, imprisonment, loss of property, or even capital punishment. Obeying the law keeps citizens out of trouble with law enforcement agencies and the judicial system.

**4. To Promote National Development and Progress**
*Explanation:* A law-abiding society attracts foreign and local investments, which leads to economic growth. Furthermore, when citizens obey laws, government resources are not wasted on fighting crime or repairing vandalized property; instead, those funds can be channeled into developmental projects like schools, hospitals, and roads.

**5. To Ensure Justice and Fairness**
*Explanation:* The law is designed to ensure that no single individual or group takes advantage of another. When everyone obeys the law, it creates a level playing field where justice prevails, contracts are honored, and the weak are protected from the oppression of the strong.

## 1. Definitions: Duties and Obligations of Citizens

In civics, the terms "duties" and "obligations" are often used interchangeably, but they have distinct meanings based on moral expectations versus legal requirements:

*   **Duties (Civic Responsibilities):** These are moral or ethical expectations that citizens should fulfill to contribute positively to society. While they are not usually enforced by law, a healthy and functioning society relies on citizens voluntarily performing these duties. Examples include voting, volunteering, and respecting the rights of others.
*   **Obligations (Legal Requirements):** These are mandatory actions that citizens are legally bound to perform as dictated by the government or the constitution. Failure to fulfill these obligations usually results in legal penalties, such as fines, imprisonment, or other sanctions. Examples include paying taxes, serving on a jury, and obeying the law.

---

## 2. Six (6) Duties of a Good Citizen

A good citizen goes beyond the basic legal requirements to actively improve their country and community. Six key duties include:

1.  **Civic Participation (Voting):** Actively participating in the democratic process by voting in local and national elections to choose competent leaders and influence public policy.
2.  **Respecting the Rights of Others:** Treating fellow citizens with dignity, tolerance, and fairness, regardless of their race, religion, gender, or political beliefs.
3.  **Community Service and Volunteering:** Dedicating time and resources to help the less fortunate, support local charities, and participate in community development projects.
4.  **Environmental Stewardship:** Protecting the environment by keeping the surroundings clean, conserving natural resources, and avoiding practices that cause pollution or climate change.
5.  **Staying Informed:** Keeping up to date with current events, understanding government policies, and holding leaders accountable through constructive criticism and dialogue.
6.  **Promoting National Unity:** Fostering patriotism and peace by standing against discrimination, supporting national symbols, and working towards the common good rather than personal or sectional interests.

    *   **Explanation:** Citizens and businesses are legally required to pay taxes (such as income tax, property tax, and VAT). This revenue is the government's primary source of funding for public services like education, healthcare, infrastructure, and security.

## 4. Five (5) Reasons Why Citizens Should Obey the Laws of the Country

1.  **To Maintain Peace and Order (Prevention of Anarchy)**
    *   **Explanation:** Laws provide a structured framework for behavior that prevents chaos and lawlessness. When everyone obeys the law, society operates smoothly, allowing people to go about their daily lives without fear of violence, theft, or disorder.
2.  **To Protect Individual Rights and Freedoms**
    *   **Explanation:** The law exists to safeguard the fundamental human rights of every citizen. By obeying laws against assault, theft, or discrimination, citizens ensure that their own rights—and the rights of their neighbors—are protected from abuse and infringement.
3.  **To Promote National Development and Economic Growth**
    *   **Explanation:** A law-abiding nation is a stable nation. Stability attracts local and foreign investment, encourages business expansion, and allows the government to focus on development projects rather than fighting internal conflicts. This leads to better infrastructure, jobs, and a higher standard of living.
4.  **To Ensure Safety and Security**
    *   **Explanation:** Many laws, such as traffic regulations and building codes, are designed specifically to protect human life. Obeying these laws reduces accidents, prevents disasters, and ensures the physical safety of all members of society.
5.  **To Avoid Legal Penalties and Uphold Justice**
    *   **Explanation:** Disobeying the law results in punishment, such as fines, imprisonment, or a criminal record. Obeying the law allows citizens to live without the fear of prosecution. Furthermore, when everyone follows the rules, it upholds the principle of "equality before the law," ensuring that the justice system remains fair for everyone.

## Evaluation 1

**1. List three periodic properties of elements that generally increase across the period of the Periodic Table.**
*   **Answer:**
    1.  **Ionization Energy:** The energy required to remove an electron increases because the nuclear charge increases and atomic radius decreases, holding electrons tighter.
    2.  **Electronegativity:** The ability of an atom to attract shared electrons increases for the same reasons (higher nuclear charge, smaller radius).
    3.  **Electron Affinity:** The energy released when an electron is added generally increases (becomes more negative) across a period.
    *(Note: Effective Nuclear Charge and Non-metallic character also increase).*

**2. Explain the term electron affinity and discuss how it varies across the period and down the group of the Periodic Table.**
*   **Definition:** Electron affinity is the amount of energy released (or change in energy) when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom to form a negative ion (anion). It represents the tendency of an atom to accept an electron.
*   **Variation across a period:** It generally **increases** (becomes more exothermic/negative) from left to right. This is because atomic size decreases and nuclear charge increases, making the nucleus more attractive to an incoming electron.
*   **Variation down a group:** It generally **decreases** (becomes less exothermic/negative) from top to bottom. This is because atomic size increases and the shielding effect of inner shells increases, reducing the attraction for an incoming electron.

# Further Mathematics (3-10-26)

The points $P(11, 7)$ divides a line segment joining $A(5, 3)$ and $B(8,5)$ externally at ratio $m : n$. Find $AP : PB$ ($m : n$)

# Solution 
$$P_x = \frac{mx_2 - nx_1}{m - n}$$
$$11 = \frac{8m - 5n}{m - n}$$
$$11(m - n) = 8m - 5n$$
$$11m - 11n = 8m - 5n$$
$$11m - 8m = 11n - 5n$$
$$3m = 6n$$
$$m = 2n$$
$$\frac{m}{n} = 2$$
$$\frac{m}{n} = \frac{2}{1}$$
$$∴ m : n = 2 : 1$$
$$AP : PB = 2 : 1$$

> [!note] Answer
> $AP : PB = 2 : 1$
